1. ADULT CPR PROCEDURE (Ages 8 and older)
If an adult is unresponsive and not breathing (or only gasping):
- Position the person flat on their back on a firm, flat surface.
- Place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest (lower half of sternum).
- Place the other hand on top, interlocking your fingers.
- Position your shoulders directly over your hands, keeping arms straight.
- Deliver chest compressions: Push down at least 2 inches (5 cm).
- Perform compressions at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute.
- After 30 compressions, perform 2 rescue breaths:
  - Tilt head back, lift chin to open the airway.
  - Pinch the nose shut, seal your mouth over theirs, and blow for 1 second.
  - Watch for chest rise. Give a second breath.
- Continue cycles of 30 compressions and 2 rescue breaths (30:2 ratio).
- Do not stop until professional medical help arrives or the person recovers.

2. CHILD AND INFANT CPR (Under 8 years old)
- For children: Use one or two hands depending on child size to compress 2 inches.
- For infants (under 1 year): Use two fingers in the center of the chest to compress
  about 1.5 inches (4 cm).
- Give 30 compressions followed by 2 rescue breaths. If you are alone and did not
  witness the collapse, perform 5 cycles (about 2 minutes) of CPR before calling
  emergency services.

3. HANDS-ONLY CPR
If you are untrained or unwilling to perform rescue breaths:
- Perform continuous chest compressions at a rate of 100-120 per minute.
- Push hard and fast in the center of the chest.
- This maintains vital blood flow to the brain and organs until help arrives.
