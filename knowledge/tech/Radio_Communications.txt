1. OVERVIEW OF RADIO SERVICES
When grid communications (cell networks, fiber internet) fail, radio frequencies
are the primary medium for regional information coordination.

2. FAMILY RADIO SERVICE (FRS) & GENERAL MOBILE RADIO SERVICE (GMRS)
- FRS: Very common hand-held walkie-talkies.
  - License: None required.
  - Frequencies: 22 channels in the UHF band (462 - 467 MHz).
  - Power limit: 2 Watts. Short range (1-2 miles line-of-sight).
- GMRS: Similar frequencies to FRS but allows higher power and external antennas.
  - License: Required (paid registration, no test).
  - Range: Up to 5-10 miles or more, supports repeaters.

3. AMATEUR (HAM) RADIO
Amateur radio offers the longest range and highest flexibility.
- License: Exam required.
- VHF (2-meter band, 144-148 MHz) & UHF (70-centimeter band, 420-450 MHz):
  - Excellent for local/regional communication.
  - National simplex calling frequency: 146.520 MHz FM.
- HF (High Frequency, 3-30 MHz):
  - Can bounce off the ionosphere (NVIS or skywave propagation) for long-distance,
    cross-country or international communications.

4. CITIZENS BAND (CB) RADIO
- Frequencies: 40 channels in the 27 MHz range (HF).
- Emergency Channel: Channel 9 (27.065 MHz) is reserved for emergency monitoring.
- Power: 4 Watts (AM), 12 Watts (SSB).

5. MESH RADIO PROTOCOLS (MESHTASTIC / LoRa)
- LoRa (Long Range) operates on sub-GHz ISM bands (915 MHz in US, 868 MHz in EU).
- Meshtastic: Open-source software running on ESP32/nRF52 microcontrollers.
- Nodes form a decentralized mesh network: each node repeats messages,
  enabling secure text communication and location sharing across miles without
  internet or cell infrastructure.
